# Pre-authorisation

## When pre-authorisation is required

Written pre-authorisation must be obtained before any of the following:

- any planned inpatient admission;
- any day-case surgical procedure;
- magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography and positron emission
  tomography scans;
- specialty biologic medicines;
- planned treatment outside the country of residence;
- any single course of treatment whose estimated cost exceeds 50,000 units.

Pre-authorisation is not required for emergency treatment. Where a member is
admitted as an emergency, the notification rules in the Claims Procedure
document apply instead.

## How to request it

A request must be submitted by the treating physician through the Aurora
provider portal and must state the proposed procedure codes, the expected length
of stay and the estimated cost. Aurora responds to a complete request within
three working days, or within twenty-four hours where the physician marks the
request as urgent.

An approval is valid for ninety days from the date of issue. If the treatment
has not begun within that period the approval lapses and a fresh request is
required. An approval states a maximum authorised amount; where the final cost
exceeds that amount by more than 15 per cent, the excess requires a separate
request and may be declined.

## Penalty for proceeding without approval

Where pre-authorisation was required and was not obtained, Aurora reduces the
eligible amount by 30 per cent before applying the deductible and coinsurance.
This penalty does not accrue toward the out-of-pocket maximum. The penalty is
waived where the member can show that the treating physician confirmed in
writing that the procedure was not one requiring pre-authorisation.

A reduction under this section is applied once per episode of care, not once per
invoice arising from that episode.

## Declined requests

Where Aurora declines a pre-authorisation request it must state the clinical or
contractual grounds for the decision in writing. A declined request may be
challenged under the Appeals and Grievances document. Proceeding with treatment
after a request has been declined means the full cost is borne by the member;
the 30 per cent reduction does not apply because no benefit is payable at all.
